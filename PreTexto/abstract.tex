\begin{abstractutfpr}%% Ambiente abstractutfpr
The automatic selection of machine learning pipelines is a central problem in AutoML, since model performance depends on preprocessing choices and algorithm combinations. This work proposes a meta-learning approach for recommending and ranking classification pipelines based on a distributional statistical representation of datasets, without neural training. In Phase 1, a meta-dataset was built from 201 OpenML datasets and 60 pipelines composed of scalers, feature selectors, and classifiers, evaluated with stratified cross-validation. Each dataset was represented by a 64-dimensional statistical vector inspired by the representation criteria of Dataset2Vec. In Phase 2, a multi-output k-nearest neighbors predictor with KDTree structure was evaluated through Leave-One-Out (LOO), using Spearman and Kendall Tau for global ranking correlation, and weighted Kendall Tau and NDCG as complementary top-ranking metrics. For $k=10$, the predictor achieved a mean Spearman correlation of $0.58 \pm 0.36$, Kendall of $0.48 \pm 0.34$, and weighted Kendall of $0.52 \pm 0.35$, indicating its ability to capture the relative ordering of pipelines, especially at the top positions. A held-out dataset demonstration is reported only as an operational example of the recommender and does not replace the statistical LOO validation. The correlation between representation dimensions and weighted Kendall ($p < 0.0001$) suggests that the statistical vector captures structure relevant to recommendation. The results indicate that statistical representations combined with k-NN provide a viable and reproducible approach to AutoML pipeline recommendation.
\end{abstractutfpr}
