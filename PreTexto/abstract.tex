\begin{abstractutfpr}
Classification pipeline selection depends on dataset characteristics and preprocessing choices. This work investigates pipeline ranking through meta-learning with a deterministic statistical representation of labeled datasets. The historical archive contains 201 OpenML tasks, 60 pipelines and 64-position vectors comprising 55 descriptors and nine padded zeros. An audit identified related tasks and eight cases that failed a minimum of five observations per class. The primary evaluation uses 193 tasks in 165 groups, five outer partitions and group-based inner validation for neighborhood selection. Multi-output k-NN and Random Forest are compared with global-mean and random-ranking baselines using Spearman, Kendall, weighted Kendall, NDCG and regret. Group-averaged weighted Kendall was $0.3995$ for k-NN, $0.4309$ for Random Forest and $0.3690$ for the global mean. The paired Random Forest minus k-NN difference was $0.0314$, with a descriptive 95\% interval of $[0.0073;0.0564]$; the k-NN advantage over the global mean remained uncertain. Error analysis found 17 tasks with nonpositive weighted correlation and one undefined case. A separate hyperparameter pilot performed 180 evaluations on raw data with preprocessing fitted within folds. The results characterize predictor effects in this benchmark while acknowledging the limitations of historical performance scores, retrospective evaluation and the absence of a complete Phase 1 reconstruction.
\end{abstractutfpr}
