\begin{abstractutfpr}
Classification pipeline selection depends on dataset characteristics and preprocessing choices. This work investigates pipeline ranking through meta-learning with deterministic statistical descriptors of labeled datasets. An archive of 201 OpenML tasks and 60 pipelines yields 193 eligible tasks in 165 groups after auditing. Phase 1 was fully reconstructed in 57900 model fits, with imputation, encoding, scaling and feature selection fitted exclusively on each training fold. All classifiers, including SVM, use identical training and test rows. Each dataset vector comprises 55 descriptors, nine padded zeros and L2 normalization. Five outer group partitions and inner neighborhood selection compare k-NN, Random Forest, global-mean and random-ranking baselines. Group-averaged weighted Kendall is 0.4280 for k-NN, 0.4768 for Random Forest and 0.4011 for the global mean. The Random Forest minus k-NN difference is 0.0515, with a descriptive 95\% interval [0.0255, 0.0771]. Controlled comparisons retain tasks and partitions while replacing historical scores and recomputing descriptors. Error analysis identifies 13 tasks with nonpositive weighted correlation and 16 undefined cases. Artifacts include predictions, statuses, parameters, row indices and checksums. The evaluation remains retrospective and its intervals descriptive; expanding the pipeline arsenal and external validation remain future work.
\end{abstractutfpr}
